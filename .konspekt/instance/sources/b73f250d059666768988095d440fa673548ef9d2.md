**Claude:** "[…] Four open items in §5, of which one is a genuine fork: **who assembles the bundle** — the alliance at publication (manifest is issuer-signed, one more thing to verify) or the bank's resolver ③ at ingest (manifest is local, ③ grows). That changes what ③ is."

**User:** Perhaps the vendor creates the bundle and alliance signs before testing?

**Claude:** "[…] **The timing doesn't work.** The bundle has to carry L8 — the quorum's VSAs are the only thing in it that converts claims into evidence. If the alliance signs before testing, the thing being signed contains L-1 and L0–L7 only. That's not the artifact that crosses the perimeter; it's the maker's submission. **And the signature is the wrong instrument.** An alliance signature over a maker-assembled container attests to *'this is the set the vendor handed us'* — provenance of receipt, not correctness of contents. It's authentication dressed as verification […]. Worse, it's actively hazardous: a signed container invites exactly the inference the schematic's one-gap panel warns about […]. **But the instinct underneath is right.** You want the delivery frozen before testing starts, so the maker can't adjust what it submitted after seeing how verification went. That's the same loophole L-1 closes for the test corpus, one layer out. So I'd split it in two: **Delivery manifest** — maker-assembled at the Delivered transition. L-1 + L0–L7 […]. **Evidence bundle** — assembled after the quorum, adding L8, the compiled layout, the policy bundle, the trust-root snapshot and the log checkpoints. […] For the freeze, use a **transparency-log entry, not a signature.** […] That gives 'this exact set existed at time T' […] and it asserts no authority over correctness […]. **Which dissolves the assembly question.** If the bundle carries no container signature, assembly confers no authority […] the resolver ③ can build it locally at ingest, and ③ stays a resolver rather than growing into a trusted packager. […] **the bundle is a container, never a credential.**"
