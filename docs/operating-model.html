code_tracer · the operating layer

Operating model

Four axes already describe this system — the evidence chain, the project machine, the package and product machines, and the data model — with two further documents covering the perimeter interface and the physical stores. Between them they cover what it means, where the bytes live, and when each write happens. This one covers who is on the hook, by when, and what each activity leaves behind. Twenty-nine activities, four parties, one perimeter.

review: proposed rev 1 · 29 Jul 2026 rolling — sections added in place renders offline · no fetched assets
One · the organising principle

Every activity leaves a trace the machinery already defines

A duty whose only output is that somebody looked cannot be audited and cannot be shown to have lapsed. So each of the twenty-nine activities below terminates in one of four things: a signed record, a transparency-log entry, a hash-chained ledger event, or a projection that visibly ages. Nothing in this model asks an operator to agree in private.

01

Evidence keeps; trust material does not

A sealed evidence set never expires: one from last year proves exactly what it proved then. What ages is the material used to check the signatures on it — who counts as a current verifier, and the log proofs. So the evidence is assembled once for the whole membership, and each bank re-checks it against material current at its own gate. That is why some duties happen once at the alliance and others repeat at every member.

02

Failure is loud by construction

Expired trust metadata hard-fails closed, stale material is a named-rule reject, a single dissent is disqualifying. The mechanism already makes bad evidence noisy. The operating model's own job is the remaining class: a duty that quietly goes unperformed.

03

The gate is identical everywhere

Team and solo modes, alliance and delegated trust paths, first admission and re-admission all face the same sixteen rules. What differs is who is accountable for having commissioned the work, recorded in the verdict as the trust path.

Mechanism, policy, and which layer this is. The architecture is explicit about its own boundary: it builds the mechanism and leaves alliance governance policy — trust-root membership, verifier admission criteria, the compromise procedure — to the alliance, on the grounds that those are not engineering questions. This document sits a layer above, where that boundary does not hold. An operating model with no cadence, no threshold and no service level cannot be run, so it proposes values and names who ratifies each one. What it never does is present a proposed value as a ratified one: every unsettled number here is marked, with its owner, in section eight.
Sealing is separate from signing. Containers are sealed and logged; they carry no signature of their own, and sealing confers no authority on the contents. Every element inside is independently signed. This is what lets the issuer assemble the shared evidence core without becoming a trust role, and it is why the delivery manifest is frozen by a log entry — an inclusion proof establishes that a set existed at a moment, and cannot be misread as an endorsement.
Two · roles

Four parties, and the functions inside them

The design names actors by role rather than by company. Operating them needs a decomposition one level down, because most of these functions are separately staffed and separately keyed. The four parties are numbered #1 to #4 and referenced by number on the chart and throughout the register; the circled numerals ①–⑨ are something else entirely — they are the nine components built here, and they belong to whichever party operates them.

Functions and activities do not correspond one to one. A function is a standing unit with an identity and a signing key; an activity is one thing done once per project or on a timer. The exposure owner performs four activities across three grains, and D3 needs two functions at once. The column above is there so coverage can be checked in both directions: every one of the twenty-nine activities has at least one function against it, and no function sits idle. Running it is what broke the old E3: three issuer functions — root custodian, log operator, index operator — appeared nowhere except that one box, which was carrying nine duties across six functions on four cadences. It is now the six-activity band at the foot of the chart, and each of those functions owns something named.
Every member is both a verifier and a bank. If one team holds both functions, that member's own verification summary and its own admission verdict rest on the same people and the same keys, and independence is nominal even though the arithmetic still reads k-of-n. Proposed as an invariant: the two functions are separately owned and separately keyed, and the registry entry carries the verification identity alone.
Three · the flow

Twenty-nine activities, nine rows and a standing band

Reading order is left to right, then down. Each box names what it acts on and what it leaves behind. Colour is the party; a crimson edge marks the components built here. The band at the foot runs on timers, with no package in front of it.

The fork sits at D2, not D3. Verification produces the verdict; admit and refuse are the two ways one evaluation terminates. Branching from D3 would imply refusal happens after admission.

Rows one and two · demand and commissioning

The first actor in the whole model is a member bank, working from its own exposure data. Solo mode skips N2 and N3 and keeps everything else.

N1

Nominate

product nominated
Trigger
Exposure data shows a product the estate cannot upgrade away from, or an application team asks for a project with no upstream baseline.
Actor
#4 Bank — exposure owner, with second line countersigning the contribution commitment.
Inputs
Ledger projections; application records from the configuration database; advisory findings; the live projection of package and release line over accepted contracts.
Action
File a signed, expiring interest declaration naming subject, driver, priority band, desired timeline and committed contribution.
Trace
Signed interest declaration, logged.
State effect
None — no project exists yet.
Service level
Declarations expire; a lapsed one is filed afresh.
Exit on failure
Expiry without seconding: the subject leaves the pipeline and returns only on a new declaration.
N2

Second

project backlogged
Trigger
An open, unexpired declaration.
Actor
#1 Other members in alliance capacity; the secretariat tallies.
Inputs
The set of open declarations.
Action
Members second the declarations they share. Those crossing the seconding threshold enter the project backlog.
Trace
Seconding records. Backlog membership is a projection over them.
State effect
None — still pre-project.
Service level
Tally inside the declaration's own expiry window.
Exit on failure
Threshold unmet at expiry: drops, as in N1. Threshold value is unset — open item 3.
N3

Prioritise

backlog ranked
Trigger
A non-empty backlog at the ballot cadence.
Actor
#1 Secretariat runs it; members vote, one member one vote.
Inputs
The backlog, and the benefits records produced by N5 in earlier cycles.
Action
Rank the backlog.
Trace
Ballot record carrying the resulting ranking.
State effect
None.
Service level
The ballot cadence — unset, open item 4. An exploited-in-the-wild flaw cannot wait for a quarterly ballot, so an emergency route with later ratification is proposed alongside it.
Exit on failure
Voter quorum unmet: the previous ranking stands unchanged.
N4

Commission

mandate signed
Trigger
A ranked entry reaches the commissioning ballot, or a sponsoring bank authorises alone in solo mode. Renewals re-enter here.
Actor
#1 Secretariat with a contribution-weighted ballot, or the #4 sponsoring bank.
Inputs
Ranked backlog; contribution register; membership terms.
Action
Vote the project in and record the authorisation, fixing mode, sponsor and trust path.
Trace
Signed mandate record; its digest becomes the governance mandate reference inside the contract.
State effect
The project may be drafted.
Service level
None.
Exit on failure
Not carried: returns to the backlog at its rank. Weighting basis is unset — open item 3.
A2

Issue

contract signed
Trigger
Mandate recorded.
Actor
#1 Issuer — contract author, signing with the issuer key.
Inputs
Mandate digest; the baseline artifact at its public coordinates; the upstream commit; advisory records for each flaw in scope; the test corpus; the live package-and-line projection; the active verifier count.
Action
Author governance, scope, baseline, corpus, schedule and release line; compute the quorum size as the greater of three and a third of the active set, and write it in; snapshot each advisory record for pinning; sign and issue. The line judgement is explicit and defaults to nothing, because continuing a lineage and starting one are different decisions.
Trace
Signed contract statement, digest minted, published to the master repository and logged.
State effect
Project drafted → proposed.
Service level
None.
Exit on failure
Provider negotiates: back to drafted. Issuer retracts: withdrawn, terminal. A mis-picked line surfaces as an admission failure at D2 rather than as silent drift.
A2a

Countersign

contract frozen — the hinge
Trigger
A proposed contract reaches the named provider.
Actor
#2 Provider.
Inputs
The signed contract; the provider's own feasibility assessment.
Action
Countersign, accepting scope, baseline, corpus, schedule, quorum size and governance. This is the second of the two signatures on the contract and the moment the terms stop being negotiable.
Trace
Countersignature over the contract digest.
State effect
Project proposed → accepted. Scope, baseline and acceptance criteria become immutable; the package machine may open.
Service level
Inside the offer window — unset, open item 2.
Exit on failure
No countersignature before the window closes: back to drafted, or withdrawn. How the provider was chosen is unowned — open item 1.

Rows three and four · production and independent verification

Everything here runs on somebody else's infrastructure. The bank observes it and controls none of it, which is why a package resting in these rows is nobody's queue at the bank to clear.

B1

Build

package built · L0–L7 signed
Trigger
Contract accepted.
Actor
#2 Provider build infrastructure.
Inputs
The contract; the baseline artifact from the public registry; upstream source at the named commit; the recipe set or patch; a pinned toolchain.
Action
Apply the transformation, build the hardened artifact, mint the eight provider claims covering baseline, flaw assessment, transformation, build provenance, test results, proof of fix, parts list and flaw status.
Trace
Eight signed statements attached as blobs and logged; the hardened digest and its suffixed package identifier.
State effect
Package accepted → built.
Service level
The contract's delivery date.
Exit on failure
A shortfall against scope routes through amendment before the delivery date — a new contract version with a fresh countersignature, so delivery is complete against a contract that tells the truth. Nothing delivered by the date: project lapsed, terminal.
B2

Hand off

delivery manifest logged
Trigger
The provider claims are minted and resolvable.
Actor
#2 Provider.
Inputs
The contract and the eight claims; the contract's list of required attestations.
Action
Assemble the delivery manifest, check it mechanically for completeness against the required list, and log its digest. This closes the loophole one layer out from the corpus freeze: what was submitted cannot be adjusted after seeing how verification went.
Trace
Delivery manifest plus its log inclusion proof. No signature — the entry establishes that this exact set existed at a moment and says nothing about whether it is correct.
State effect
Project accepted → delivered.
Service level
The delivery date.
Exit on failure
Incomplete against the required list: delivery does not stand, and the delivery clock keeps running.
C1

Rebuild

package rebuilt · digest compared
Trigger
Delivery, plus an assignment naming this verifier.
Actor
#3 Each assigned verifier's verification operator.
Inputs
Delivery manifest; the contract; the baseline from the public registry; the pinned toolchain; the distributable rebuild harness at its current version.
Action
Rebuild from the named source and transformation, then compare the resulting digest with the hardened digest. Reproducibility is what lets independent parties converge without coordinating.
Trace
Rebuild result, held for C3.
State effect
None until C3.
Service level
From delivery — proposed at five business days, open item 2.
Exit on failure
Digest mismatch is dissent, which disqualifies rather than being outvoted. The project freezes pending investigation.
C2

Re-run tests

results compared
Trigger
Rebuild complete.
Actor
#3 The same verification operator.
Inputs
The corpus named by digest in the contract; the supplemental suite; the provider's test and proof claims; a proof case for each flaw in scope.
Action
Re-run the corpus and the proofs; confirm each proof fails on the baseline and passes on the hardened build; check interface stability and coverage of the changed code against the contract's thresholds; compare everything with what the provider claimed.
Trace
Result set, held for C3.
State effect
None until C3.
Service level
Inside the same window as C1.
Exit on failure
Divergence from the provider's claims is dissent, handled as in C1.
C3

Attest

verification summary signed
Trigger
C1 and C2 complete for this verifier.
Actor
#3 The same operator, signing with the verification identity held in the registry.
Inputs
Rebuild and test results; the contract; the log tree head observed at signing time.
Action
Publish a verification summary asserting the digest match, the re-checked results, the policy pass, and conformance against the named contract version.
Trace
Signed summary, logged, carrying the observed tree head — which is what makes a split view of the log detectable later.
State effect
Once the required number exist with zero dissent: package built → verified, and project delivered → fulfilled.
Service level
As C1.
Exit on failure
Too few summaries by the delivery date: project lapsed. Any conformance breach: breached, terminal. Any dissent: frozen for investigation, since a lone disagreement is either a defect in the toolchain specification or something worse, and both need saying out loud.

Row five · sealing and publication

Two boxes the graph and the on-screen explainer both under-specify. A3 creates the shared identifier that lets two members cite one evidence set; A4 is the only activity whose behaviour differs by governance mode.

A3

Seal core

evidence core sealed and logged
Trigger
The project reaches fulfilled.
Actor
#1 Issuer — core assembler.
Inputs
Delivery manifest and its proof; the contract; the eight provider claims and every verification summary; the compiled layout with its compiler version; the policy bundle with its version; one pinned advisory snapshot per flaw in scope.
Action
Assemble the core as one canonical file listing each element's digest and locator, compute the core digest, and log it. Assembled once for the whole membership, because assembling it per bank would produce many identifiers over one evidence set and then no two members could cite the same thing.
Trace
The canonical core file in the master repository plus its log entry. Sealed and logged, carrying no signature of its own.
State effect
None in any machine. The core is the precondition for the crossing.
Service level
Same day as fulfilment — proposed, open item 2.
Exit on failure
Any element missing or not resolvable by digest: no core, and the package cannot cross the perimeter.
A4

Publish

core published · blessed or petitioned
Trigger
Core sealed.
Actor
#1 Issuer — registrar for the trust path, secretariat where a petition is needed.
Inputs
The core; the governance mode and trust path frozen in the contract.
Action
Team output publishes to the shared distribution point under the alliance root, carrying blessing by definition. Solo output stays on its sponsor's delegated path until a blessing petition meets the quorum threshold.
Trace
Publication record, and a petition record where one applies.
State effect
None. It determines which members can discover the core on shared rails.
Service level
None.
Exit on failure
Petition fails: the core stays on the sponsor's delegated path. Another member may still consume it, carrying the commissioning judgement as its own — the evidence is identical, and only the question of whether the work was worth commissioning was one member's.

Row six · the gate

Everything from here runs inside the bank, offline. Resolution is the last activity that touches a network.

D1

Resolve

ingest envelope sealed · last online step
Trigger
A package reaches verified and its core digest is published.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator, running the resolver.
Inputs
The artifact digest; the attestation index; the published core; local trust material; the artifact's location in the quarantine registry.
Action
Discover the attestation set through the index, fetch the core, obtain a current tree head with consistency proofs against the heads frozen in the verification summaries, and seal the envelope over the core digest. The index is a lookup and never evidence, so a wrong index fails immediately against signed, digest-bound records. Carry each pinned advisory snapshot through verbatim: rule 16 digests those bytes as they are carried, so reformatting, re-indenting or re-encoding during assembly invalidates them in both the registry and the tar form.
Trace
The sealed envelope, identified by its own digest, held in the bank's bundle store.
State effect
Package verified → resolved.
Service level
None on the step. The material it seals carries the windows, and they are measured at D2.
Exit on failure
Incomplete index lookup: a reconciliation record against the index operator, retried after replay. Divergent tree heads across the quorum: the split-view path, disqualifying, no envelope sealed. Material outside its window: refresh and re-seal.
D2

Verify

sixteen rules checked, offline
Trigger
Envelope sealed.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator. The runner is stateless.
Inputs
One envelope and the core it names, and nothing else. Anything the gate needs from elsewhere would be a live dependency.
Action
Recompile the layout locally from the signed contract and compare digests; run the structural pass over the layout for the right steps, right signers, thresholds met and graph intact; run the semantic pass for meaning — flaws fixed covering scope, digest agreement across the quorum, coverage above threshold, conformance full, freshness inside limits, and every in-scope flaw carrying a pinned advisory snapshot whose bytes match the digest the contract recorded and whose affected range agrees with what the contract asserts. Structure and meaning are two passes because neither alone is sufficient.
Trace
Rule-by-rule evaluation, carried into the verdict at D3 or D3a.
State effect
Package resolved → gate.
Service level
None on the decision, which takes as long as the evidence requires. Queue age from resolution is the figure that is measured.
Exit on failure
Any rule failing routes to D3a with the rule named.
D3

Admit

L9 and verdict signed · promoted
Trigger
Structural and semantic passes both hold.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator running the harness; the application team owns the suite inside it.
Inputs
The bank's own acceptance suite, executed as an inspection during verification. The layout may require that an inspection by this name exists and never supplies its command.
Action
Fold the acceptance result into the same verdict; on admission, promote the artifact to the golden repository, which is the only path to consumption.
Trace
Signed acceptance record, which never leaves the bank; an immutable verdict pinning the core digest, the envelope digest, the trust-root version and the trust path; and the suite digest, so that a suite quietly ceasing to exercise the package becomes visible.
State effect
Package gate → adopted.
Service level
None on the decision; queue age measured.
Exit on failure
Acceptance failure routes to D3a like any other rule.

Row seven · refusal

The half the on-screen explainer has yet to draw. Conformance is binary, so this row is where an honest shortfall becomes visible instead of being softened into a passing verdict.

D3a

Refuse

rejected · failing rule named
Trigger
Any of the sixteen rules fails, dissent is present, or acceptance fails.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator.
Inputs
The evaluation from D2.
Action
Record the verdict naming the failing rule. The artifact stays in the quarantine registry, whose only reader is the gate — any other reader would let a team consume something unadmitted.
Trace
Signed verdict, decision refused, failing rule recorded.
State effect
Package gate → rejected, terminal. A corrected resubmission is a new package under a new project.
Service level
None.
Exit on failure
Routes to D3b, or the application team does without.
D3b

Accept risk

acceptance signed against a rule
Trigger
A refused package the bank still needs, or the standing finding that no warranty is offered.
Actor
#4 Bank — second line.
Inputs
The refusing verdict and the rule it names.
Action
Record an explicit acceptance citing that rule. No weaker admission tier exists, in any mode or on any trust path, which is what keeps the shortfall loud instead of load-bearing and forgotten three years later.
Trace
Signed risk acceptance citing the verdict digest and the rule, carrying its own review date.
State effect
None. The package remains refused.
Service level
The review date on the acceptance.
Exit on failure
Not available as a console write path until version two; the record shape exists now, and the same finding covers the absence of a warranty.

Rows eight and nine · operation and closure

The bridge is temporary by design, so this is where the model spends most of its calendar time. Expiry is arithmetic over the current date, never a stored status.

E1

Open exposure

product active · issued event written
Trigger
Adoption, and the package deployed.
Actor
#4 Bank — exposure owner, through the tracker.
Inputs
The verdict; application records; the bridge expiry from the contract schedule; the flaws the contract covers.
Action
Write the issued event and open a row against the application and the lineage. The bridge was built and checked once, so the per-application dimension lives only here.
Trace
A hash-chained ledger event. The exposure row and the state pointer are projections replayed from the chain, which is how the estate can answer what was in force on a past date and what it covered.
State effect
Product → active. This is the handover, and the seam between digest-keyed records and lineage-keyed ones.
Service level
Same day as deployment.
Exit on failure
A deployment the configuration database knows and the ledger does not is an untracked bridge, caught by the monthly reconciliation at S6.
E2

Review

product expiring · retire or renew
Trigger
Expiry falls inside the notice window, computed at read time.
Actor
#4 Bank — exposure owner, with second line on the decision record.
Inputs
The exposure row; the stated destination release; upstream availability; the application team's migration capacity.
Action
Decide retire or renew, per application.
Trace
The decision, and either a migration commitment for E1a or a fresh commissioning at N4.
State effect
Product active → sunset at lapse, whichever way the decision goes.
Service level
Decided before the notice window closes. The window is the sum of the service levels on the renewal path plus buffer, so it is derived rather than picked — open item 2.
Exit on failure
No decision by lapse: sunset with the exposure still open, which the debt figures count and name.
E1a

Migrate

product retired · exposure closed
Trigger
Retirement decided.
Actor
#4 Bank — application team.
Inputs
The destination release named on the exposure row; the bank's release train.
Action
Move the application onto the upstream-fixed release and drop the bridge. This is the only activity in the model that actually removes an exposure.
Trace
A retired event closing the row.
State effect
Product sunset → retired, terminal.
Service level
The bridge expiry date.
Exit on failure
Not migrated by expiry: the exposure sits past expiry and undecided, which is the figure the risk function watches.
N5

Benefits

project closed against its mandate
Trigger
Every exposure under one project closed, or its bridge expiry reached.
Actor
#4 Bank — exposure owner. The secretariat consumes the result.
Inputs
The mandate record; the flaws the contract covered; exposure history; migration outcomes.
Action
Record what the project delivered against what its mandate claimed. Without this the next prioritisation ballot runs with no evidence of what the last one achieved.
Trace
A benefits record citing the mandate reference and the closed exposures.
State effect
Project closed inside the bank. The arc that opened on a product now closes on the commitment made about it.
Service level
Within one cadence of the last exposure closing.
Exit on failure
No record: the loop back to N1 and N3 runs on opinion. What the record asserts — countable outcomes, or a judgement about spend — is open item 5.
E2a

Re-run

fresh verdict · same core digest
Trigger
On demand: a revoked verifier key, a refreshed envelope, a supervisor's request, or the standing replay drill.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator; second line for the drill.
Inputs
The stored core and a freshly sealed envelope over it. A sealed set from last year still proves what it proved then, so it can be re-admitted against current trust material.
Action
Re-run the gate offline from stored inputs and compare. Two runs from the same inputs must produce the same verdict, byte for byte.
Trace
A fresh verdict over the same core digest with a new envelope digest. The earlier verdict is never edited.
State effect
None, unless the outcome differs — which is itself the finding.
Service level
The drill runs twice a year; on-demand runs are unbounded.
Exit on failure
A differing outcome is investigated as a finding. A core no longer retrievable makes the claim of offline re-runnability false, which is open item 6.

The standing band · no package in front of them

Six duties on timers rather than on deliveries. They were a single box until the coverage check found three issuer functions appearing nowhere else, which is how a bucket hides work nobody has been asked to staff. Every other activity in this register waits for something to arrive; these run whether or not anything does.

S1

Trust metadata

metadata re-signed before expiry
Trigger
Each role's metadata approaching its expiry, and any suspected compromise.
Actor
#1 Issuer — root custodian, with the full co-signing threshold for a change to the root itself.
Inputs
Current metadata versions; the registry snapshot; co-signing keys, held across the alliance and its member banks rather than in one organisation.
Action
Re-sign the short-lived metadata on its own cadence, and run the rotation ceremony annually and on compromise. The threshold spans several organisations so that no single one disappearing can strand it.
Trace
A new metadata version, published as static files.
State effect
None.
Service level
Ahead of each expiry, by a margin.
Exit on failure
Metadata allowed to expire fails every client closed and verification stops entirely. Of every duty here this is the one whose omission is most expensive, and it is invisible until the day it bites.
S2

Registry and assignment

active count published
Trigger
Monthly, and each time a project reaches accepted.
Actor
#1 Issuer — registrar, component ⑨.
Inputs
Verifier entries and their attested activity; the contract digest, for assignment.
Action
Confirm each entry active or revoked and publish the active count. Then derive each project's verifier assignment from the contract digest over the registry snapshot, so that no one selects a friendly quorum and the selection can be checked afterwards by anyone holding the same snapshot.
Trace
Entry statuses, the published count, and an assignment record per project.
State effect
None directly. It sets the required quorum that is then written into each new contract.
Service level
Monthly attestation; assignment at acceptance.
Exit on failure
A stale count silently lowers the required quorum, because the number is computed from it. This is the one skipped duty in the model that the gate cannot catch, which is why it carries a published figure of its own.
S3

Log and checkpoints

tree heads published
Trigger
Continuous.
Actor
#1 Issuer — log operator.
Inputs
Entries submitted by every party that logs one: contracts, delivery manifests, verification summaries, sealed cores.
Action
Operate the append-only log and publish signed tree heads. Because one party operates it, consumers rely on heads observed independently by the quorum rather than on a single asserted head.
Trace
Signed tree heads and inclusion proofs.
State effect
None.
Service level
An availability commitment, unset. This is a real operational undertaking: a log, its database, key custody and an uptime promise.
Exit on failure
With no current head an envelope cannot be sealed at D1 and ingest stops. Divergent heads across the quorum are the split-view path.
S4

Index reconciliation

index lag published
Trigger
Daily.
Actor
#1 Issuer — index operator.
Inputs
The log, and the current index.
Action
Replay the log into the index, confirm the index is complete, and publish the lag as a number so it is visible rather than assumed.
Trace
A reconciliation record carrying the lag.
State effect
None. The index is a lookup and never evidence, so an incomplete index delays discovery and cannot admit anything wrong.
Service level
Lag at or under twenty-four hours.
Exit on failure
Omissions go unnoticed, and D1 fails to discover attestations that do exist.
S5

Trust material refresh

local material inside its windows
Trigger
The windows themselves: seven days for the trust-root snapshot, twenty-four hours for the checkpoint set.
Actor
#4 Bank — gate operator.
Inputs
Published metadata and checkpoints; the local directory that holds them, under configuration management.
Action
Refresh the local material and stamp its version. Triage the board in the same pass, leaving the columns outside the perimeter alone, since a package resting there is nobody's queue here to clear.
Trace
The refreshed directory at a recorded version, which every verdict then pins.
State effect
None. It is the precondition for sealing an envelope at all.
Service level
Tightenable by the bank, never loosenable.
Exit on failure
Stale material is a named refusal at D2 — loud, and correctly so, since the alternative is admitting against material nobody checked.
S6

Ledger reconciliation

untracked deployments named
Trigger
Monthly.
Actor
#4 Bank — exposure owner, component ⑤.
Inputs

Action

Eight · open

Seven values, and who settles each

Three owners, and the distinction is about authority rather than difficulty. alliance policy binds every member, so a bank cannot set it alone. bank policy is internal and needs no one else's agreement. engineering is ours to specify and lands in an artifact. Where a defensible default exists it is proposed; where the value encodes how much influence contribution buys, no default is offered, because that is a constitutional question wearing operational clothes.

  1. alliance policyProvider selection. Who selects the provider, and against what criteria. The mechanism obligation is ours and already satisfied: the mandate records the selection and the contract carries it, so "was this properly commissioned" stays checkable. Proposed default — the commissioning ballot selects in team mode, the sponsor selects in solo mode, and either way the mandate names the choice. Until this is answered, box A2a describes a countersignature by a party nobody has been appointed to be.
  2. alliance policybank policyService levels, then the notice window. The verification window binds members, so it is the alliance's; the offer window is the issuer's; the notice window at E2 is internal to each bank. Proposed — five business days from delivery to a verification summary, and the notice window derived as the sum of the renewal path's service levels plus buffer rather than picked.
  3. alliance policySeconding threshold and ballot weighting. No default proposed. Both values encode how much influence a contribution buys over what gets commissioned, which the membership has to decide for itself. The mechanism is indifferent to the numbers: N2 tallies against a threshold and N4 weights a ballot whatever the values are.
  4. alliance policyBallot cadence and the emergency route. Proposed — a regular cadence plus standing emergency authority with later ratification, because a flaw under active exploitation cannot wait for a scheduled vote and the alternative is commissioning outside the process entirely.
  5. bank policyalliance floorWhat a benefits record asserts. The alliance needs flaws closed and applications migrated, since those are what weight the next prioritisation ballot. Beyond that floor a bank can measure what it likes, including spend against mandate, which is a judgement rather than a count.
  6. engineeringalliance policyCore hosting and retention. Hosting is settled — the alliance master repository serves the core. The retention period is not, and the replay claim in E2a holds only as long as both the core and the envelope are held. A verdict naming a core nobody serves cannot be re-run.
  7. alliance policyengineeringVerifier and admission separation. Whether a member must own and key its verification function separately from its admission function, as a membership term. The engineering hook exists either way: the registry records the verification identity alone, so the separation is checkable once it is required.
Ours alone, and still open: whether A2 splits into separate drafting and issuance activities, given the release-line judgement has a screen of its own; how demand formation works for a project with no prior baseline, since N1 assumes exposure data a new project does not have; and which activities the reference implementation exercises, where the answer is probably the replay drill and the stale-material refusal alone. These three land in artifacts without anyone else's agreement.
One item is genuinely not ours at any layer. Which organisations sit in the trust-root threshold, how a verifier is admitted or removed, and what happens on suspected compromise. That is about which institutions hold which keys, and no operating model can propose it on the membership's behalf. The mechanism is built — a co-signed root with delegation — and the architecture names this as the largest remaining item for exactly this reason.
